However, the number of invoices tells a different story. You processed the highest number of bills (1,080) in July, but the revenue that month was only ₹32.7 lakh. This suggests a shift towards smaller-ticket transactions or different service/product mixes in certain months. The average invoice value dropped significantly from around ₹8,200 in June to just ₹1,800 in September. <strong>Action:</strong> Investigate the product/service mix and pricing strategy in low-revenue, high-volume months to protect your overall margin.</p>

                <p><strong>Purchase & Vendor Management:</strong> Your purchase activity is highly volatile. There were massive purchase outflows in the middle of the year (₹1.73 crore in June, ₹1.50 crore in July), which coincided with periods of high sales. However, purchases crashed to just ₹3.9 lakh in September. This irregular pattern can strain cash flow and supplier relationships. <strong>Action:</strong> Aim for more consistent, planned purchasing aligned with a sales forecast to smooth out cash requirements and potentially negotiate better credit terms.</p>

                <p><strong>Inventory Movement (Stock Turn):</strong> A critical red flag is visible in your inventory data. For every month from April to August, the <strong>total outward value of inventory has been significantly higher than the inward value</strong>. For example, in June, inward value was ₹1.10 crore, but outward value was ₹1.70 crore. This pattern is unsustainable and indicates you are consistently selling more stock than you are purchasing or producing. This will quickly lead to stock-outs, lost sales, and operational disruption. <strong>Action:</strong> This is a top priority. Review your inventory recording process immediately. This discrepancy could be due to data entry errors, not recording all purchases, or issues with cost valuation. You must reconcile your physical stock with book records to get a true picture and prevent business halting.</p>

                <p><strong>Operational Efficiency Check:</strong> The combination of fluctuating sales per invoice, erratic purchasing, and the alarming inventory trend points to potential gaps in your operational controls and planning. The business is generating profits (as seen in the P&L), but these ground-level inconsistencies risk its smooth day-to-day running and long-term stability.</p>
